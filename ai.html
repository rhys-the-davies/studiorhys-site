How I use AI

AI is a remarkable tool. It helps me move faster with the skills I already have, while the judgement, the decisions and the responsibility stay with me.

I use AI every day, much as a carpenter uses power tools. It takes on the slow, repetitive parts of the work and gives me time back for the parts that matter most: listening, asking good questions, and deciding what to do. The calls are still mine to make, and I'm accountable for everything I deliver.

These are the principles I work by.

I'm accountable for everything I deliver

Whatever tools helped along the way, I've read it, checked it and I stand behind it. If something is wrong, that's my mistake.

AI is never a source

Language models are fluent, and fluency can sound a lot like truth. Nothing an AI tells me counts as a fact until I've verified the source. Every number, figure and claim I make comes with a real source you can check yourself.

The thinking stays human

The questions worth asking, the patterns, the conversations that change a project's direction come from your experience and mine, from working alongside people - the result is yours.

Human-made, where it counts

If you'd like to use AI-generated images or video, that's fine, but I'll always make the case for work made and edited by humans. I believe what we make, especially art, has value simply because people made it. Anything made with AI will always be clearly labelled.

Your data stays yours

I don't put confidential client information into AI tools without your agreement. If I have to, I use tools whose terms rule out training on your data.

Your rules come first

If your organisation has its own AI policy, or you'd rather your project used no AI at all, I'll follow your lead.
